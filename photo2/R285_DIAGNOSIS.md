# R285 diagnosis — before replacement algorithms

The earlier dark-stopping intention was weakened by successive changes in what
constituted a support, rather than lost in rendering. R265's straight native paths
explicitly stopped at darkness and prohibited winding around a trench; its report
already warned that free connected growth could bypass a dark separation.
R282's selected colored C instead applies hue/saturation, a peak-relative
luminance floor, a17px radius, opening and erosion. Connectivity then stands in
for ownership. Solidity/neck retention also influence acceptance. Neither the
mask selection nor these shape tests measure a saddle between two bright regions.
R283 adds symmetric overlap/peak diagnostics but preserves those native masks.

R284A A and R284B T move farther toward geometric selection: opening removes
features that cannot contain a disk, even if their pixels are legitimate bead
material. Dilation restores a rim without testing a dark boundary. Fixed radii
can create flat/circular edges on still-bright material. T's good-looking tail
removal therefore does not establish photographic correctness; the user's rejection
is scientifically consequential and supersedes the previous recommendation.

Hue and saturation still supply a learned material/background exclusion, not
identity. Same-color neighbors satisfy it equally; shadow and neutral highlights
can violate it within one bead. Luminance in J supplies a true isophote, but its
single level is background plus18% of a nearby peak contrast. A red specular peak
can dominate that level and remove much darker valid red material. Background is
estimated in a ring around A, so an erroneous old support can contaminate J's
threshold. A weak cue yields the opposite problem: a low threshold joins multiple
features. The inherited `component` helper can even substitute a component up to
six pixels away when the selected cue is excluded; that is not preserved ownership.

R284B E does examine renewed brightness rises after a running minimum. However,
it accepts the best surviving connected route, allowing a bright detour around a
local valley. W competes between maxima of negative luminance; markers can be
multiple reflections on one body, and separate local solves can still overlap.
L uses gradient stopping and curvature but may cut notches or follow material/search
bounds. Gradient magnitude alone cannot distinguish a specular slope from a seam.

J's successful yellow result shows why the intensity field must govern growth.
The next tests retain J and examine diffuse illumination separately from peaks,
persistent saddles with evidence on both sides, and computation-domain sensitivity.
Competition must merge weakly separated features rather than treating every seed
as a bead. No shape score will select the winner; native gains/losses, local
brightness routes and independent photographic validation will decide the merits.
