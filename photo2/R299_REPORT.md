## Forward model and historical reuse

R257 supplied the half-vector lighting diagnostic, intensity-weighted half-maximum footprint, moments and finite-angular quadrature. R276 supplied reusable photographic observations and a warning about model-dependent ownership and contact failures. Neither historic radius, camera frame, lamp, nor owner is inherited.

The predictor uses the actual R298 placement and rounded annular implicit surface: inner hole, flat axial portions, outer/inner cylindrical portions and elliptical curved transitions. Radius and axial scaling come from each saved model. Analytic normals apply the inverse transpose of that scaling and the current local-to-world frame. First-hit camera rays use the actual elevation, image roll and pinhole perspective; the viewer direction is recomputed at each hit. All retained model families are supported.

Every bead is available as a camera occluder and a self/neighbor light blocker. Shadow rays use conservative bounding spheres only for acceleration, followed by the actual annular implicit surface. Finite sources stop rays at the source. Unfinished shadow rays block contributions and are counted. Camera unknowns never become surface hits. A predicted highlight requires peak intensity≥1e−4; missing highlights are explicit. Peaks maximize the sampled pure specular field; centroids and covariance use pixels at≥50% of that peak. Multiple visible lobes are retained in the footprint; a centroid can lie between them. The API accepts any set of model owners, including the full chain.

One shared isotropic Blinn half-vector lobe is fitted. No per-bead light, offset, rotation, translation, material gain or roughness is permitted. Finite positional sources include inverse-square variation. Extended sources are a single uniform-radiance angular rectangle, not multiple independently fitted lamps. Fresnel/microfacet masking, optical transmission, calibrated PSF and diffuse/specular separation are not solved; these remain material-model alternatives. The predictor is therefore physically coherent in geometry and visibility, but not a complete radiometric image-formation model.

## What the residuals diagnose

R298 directional has a trusted mean residual vector(+3.02,+0.36)px, whereas typical errors are about12px. Trial15 reduces that mean to(+1.84,+1.32)px while leaving typical errors around10px. A simple global offset would therefore leave most disagreement. Descriptive harmonic fits against projected axis orientation explain only15.5% of R298 residual variance and9.3% for trial15; phase explains8.3% and13.1%. These are descriptive fits, not additional calibration or independent validation. Whole-photo vectors, geographic means and orientation/phase ledgers are exported.

Ownership and surface placement are leading unresolved explanations.35/45 stronger observations have ambiguous positive-interior ownership under R298. The first hit at the observed peak disagrees with its frozen dominant-interior owner for14/52 cases (18/52 for trial15). Three R298 cases and four trial15 cases lie more than5px from the nearest sampled assigned visible surface. That distance is a sampling diagnostic, not a rigorous continuous-surface lower bound. Light cannot move a modeled footprint, but tiny unsampled components prevent treating this diagnostic as a proof of geometric exclusion.

Even optimistic residual-based selection among recorded competing owners leaves a median peak error9.06px for R298 and8.06px for trial15. Those are explicitly diagnostic values; no such reassignment enters any score above. At modeled first hits, the mirror-implied light direction has median mismatch99.2° for R298 and97.0° for trial15. Since those normals depend on unresolved owners, these large discrepancies do not identify a real lamp. They demonstrate incompatibility of the tested normal/owner/light combination.

A wrong global direction or finite distance alone is insufficient in the tested families. Simple camera/axis changes did not yield a useful correction; shared phase helps some cases and hurts others. Bead curvature/thickness, helix placement and ownership remain competing geometric explanations. Diffuse contamination is important for yellow and some red observations, but black median errors remain large too. The one-source half-vector material model may also be inadequate. No second light is introduced and no unique cause is claimed. The evidence rejects these tested common-light/model combinations at the requested accuracy; it does not prove that the real photograph used more than one lamp.
